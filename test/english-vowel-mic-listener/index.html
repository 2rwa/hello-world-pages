Reference profile

General American / Michigan data
The 12 reference categories follow Hillenbrand et al. (1995): heed, hid, hayed, head, had, hod, hawed, hoed, hood, who’d, hud, heard. The diphthongs /eɪ/ and /oʊ/ are shown by their steady-state reference point; the trail lets you see their movement.

Speaker calibration

Defaults work immediately, but English vowel spaces vary strongly by speaker and dialect. Tap a card and sustain the target vowel for about 1.4 seconds to replace that reference with your own F1/F2/F3 median.
Sensitivity / implementation notes
0.008
Audio stays in the browser. Analysis uses downsampling to 12 kHz, pre-emphasis, Hann windowing, 18th-order LPC, and peak picking for F1–F3. This is a formant classifier, not speech recognition: consonants, whispering, nasalization, background noise, and strong dialect shifts can produce wrong labels. /ɝ/ gets extra F3 weighting because rhoticity is strongly associated with a lowered third formant.
